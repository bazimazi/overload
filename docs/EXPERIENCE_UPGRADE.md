# Experience upgrade — 8 October 2026

The implemented progression model was more complete than the experience of playing it. This pass builds on the existing uncommitted pixel-art work and addresses the most visible gaps without replacing the Overload/Override rules, exact endless counters or atomic checkpoint transactions.

| Review finding | Implemented improvement |
| --- | --- |
| Simple pursuit and perpendicular steering could leave enemies parked on cover. | Footprint-aware A* routes, cached room graphs, staggered repathing, smooth route segments, ranged retreat/orbit and separation steering. Three simultaneous windups and the existing heavy budget bound pack pressure. |
| Walk cycles were excessively fast; attacks snapped between sparse poses. | Distance-driven cadence, small snapped step/breath offsets, windup anticipation, phase-driven follow-through, recoil, spawn rings, death fade, traverse afterimages and visible stagger stars. Damage timing remains fixed-tick authority. |
| Basic hit numbers failed to distinguish important combat events. | Bounded critical-hit tracking, distinct critical numbers, stagger feedback, boss windup/recovery/opening captions, low-Life/flask guidance and deliberate-action failure notices. |
| The defining mechanic was explained mainly in a field-guide menu. | Contextual first-room coaching observes movement, consumed Momentum, actual signature selection and Traverse. Live action predictions and signature-ready borders remain read-only. |
| Each ordinary room forced another reward modal. | Rewards commit first, then an animated exit opens in the room. Walk into it, Continue with G / D-pad up, click Continue, or equip-and-continue with Q / X. Pause exposes the comparison and Hearth route. The campaign finale retains its ending screen. |
| Loot and advancement were difficult to evaluate while continuing. | Real transaction deltas, level-up and available-point disclosure, base-stat comparison, gear affixes and saved equip-and-continue. No extra reward transaction is generated by equipping. |
| Binding selection required cycling blind through a button. | A controller-accessible signature picker shows memory requirements, costs and plain-language behavior before the complete build is saved. |
| Hearth was the combat floor behind another menu. | A new original AI-generated sanctuary, warm light, perimeter embers, animated atmosphere, Frame portraits and visible regional progress. The main menu fits at 1280×720 with 100% text; smaller/enlarged menus scroll. |
| Ground hazards and world objectives used plain blocks/circles. | Filling floor-warning clocks, repeated slash markers, reduced-flash-aware pulses, a spectral breakable ward and pixel-art keystone chest. Their damage and collision checks still use the existing authored geometry. |
| One looping track carried every scene. | An original synchronized percussion layer fades in for combat, rises for bosses, falls at checkpoints, and ducks during pause. Major tells receive a rate-limited warning cue. |

## Art and rendering

Twelve original/runtime PNG pairs and 328 atlas frames are retained in `assets/source/pixel-v2` and `src/Overload.Game/Assets/Pixel`. The new Hearth asset was generated with the built-in image_gen tool; its exact prompt and workspace paths are in `generation.json`. Earlier generated sheets are preserved. Alpha-silhouette inspection produces measured source rectangles and foot pivots, with hashes for reproducible imports. The static prop atlas now has valid static animation metadata. Runtime loading also rejects missing/out-of-range animation rows.

`python build/validate-pixel-assets.py` checks source/runtime equality, real transparency for every sheet, frame bounds, pivots and animation rows. World art uses the 640×360 SubViewport, nearest filtering and integer output scaling; menus and text render at output resolution. Source art is not silently resized or repainted. See `src/Overload.Game/Assets/MANIFEST.md` for provenance and the four earlier sheets whose prompts were not recorded.

## Remaining quality limits

This is a substantial playable upgrade, not a claim of a finished world-class commercial RPG. Existing generated sheets provide finite poses; anticipation and interpolation improve their motion, but they do not replace artist-directed eight-direction animation production. The campaign still uses the existing sixteen authored checkpoints and finite encounter templates. No new claim is made about 8–12 hours of campaign content, 36 talents per Frame, physical-controller feel, final audio mastering, broad hardware performance or player enjoyment. Those targets require further content work and real playtesting. Automated tests demonstrate behavior and regression safety; they cannot establish the subjective quality bar.

The current development-machine rendered stress sample uses Compatibility rendering, 1920×1080 output, 60 enemies, the 200-projectile cap and a 30-second warmup. Its timing report records the exact measured duration and percentiles; it does not substitute for the earlier five-minute Vulkan sample or additional hardware testing.
